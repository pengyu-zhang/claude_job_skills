Dear hiring team,

I am a PhD candidate in Computer Science at Northfield University, and I am applying for the Machine Learning Engineer position in the Forecasting team at Example Energy. I have finished writing my thesis and can start in March 2027.

The vacancy says the team "keeps demand forecasts reliable when customer behaviour changes". This is the problem I have studied for four years. My research is about forecasting models that are trained on past seasons and then have to work through weather and tariff changes they have never seen.

First, I built a load forecasting model called GridCast. It separates the slow seasonal pattern from fast local changes, so only the fast part needs retraining when the data shifts. On load data from about 12,000 smart meters, the error at a one-year gap is 12% lower than the strongest baseline. Second, I built a monitor called DriftWatch that estimates forecast error from the input statistics alone, because the true values arrive days later. Both projects have public code, and GridCast comes with a public benchmark. The idea I would bring to your team is the same in both: decide in advance which part of a model is allowed to change, and watch the inputs before the errors arrive.

I have also worked with data from other groups. With a colleague who led the project, we collected ten years of river gauge data and defined forecasting tasks with fixed splits, so that different models can be compared fairly.

This paragraph is about the gaps. I have no industry experience, and my models have run in research projects, where nobody depends on the forecast the next morning. I have not worked with streaming systems or with the cloud platform named in the vacancy. I learn tools quickly when I have a concrete task, and I would use the first weeks to learn how the team deploys and monitors its models.

Thank you for considering my application. I look forward to hearing from you.
